What the firm backs

Good businesses constrained by infrastructure, not by demand.

A founder-owned company at $1MM or more of EBITDA generally has the hard part already solved. Real customers, proven unit economics, a brand that means something in its market.

What it often lacks is the machinery to scale efficiently. Processes designed for a ten-person team. Sales that depend on what a few people remember. Headcount that rises in step with revenue because nothing in the operation compounds.

That is a solvable problem, and solving it is what the principals have done for thirty years — most recently with AI, which is unusually well suited to the repetitive coordination work that consumes capacity in businesses of this size.

How most relationships begin

Frequently with work, not with a term sheet.

Alan Warms and Gary Leff run an advisory practice, OVD Advisory, that spends two to three weeks inside a business establishing where AI creates value, what each initiative returns, and in what order the work should be done.

Many of those engagements end there, and should. The document belongs to the owner. Where both parties come to agree on what the business could be worth, and both want to pursue it together, a conversation about capital follows — never as the stated purpose of the engagement.

It is an unusually good way to meet. Both sides have seen how the other works before anyone discusses price.
