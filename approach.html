Approach

Operators first, investors second.

The principals ran companies for two decades before they invested in any. That order shapes what OVD Capital does after closing, and it is the reason the firm buys few things and works on them closely.

Sequence

Measurement before optimization.

The instinct after a close is to start improving things. That is usually wrong, because nothing can be optimized that is not first measured, and in a founder-owned business of this size the measurement is frequently the weakest part of the operation.

First, the financial foundation

Clean accounting on a consistent basis. A single source of truth for customer data. A forecast the management team believes and a discipline for allocating capital against it. This is unglamorous and it precedes everything else.

Then, capacity

Most mid-market businesses are constrained by hours rather than by demand. The work is to find where those hours go, which of it repeats, and what can be absorbed without changing what customers experience. AI is well suited to this and badly suited to a great deal else, and the discipline is knowing the difference.

Then, growth

With the foundations in place and capacity returned to the people who generate revenue, growth becomes a question of where to point it. Sometimes that includes acquisitions; a business running on modern infrastructure is the natural home for books of business belonging to firms that are not.

Method

What the firm does not do.

Replace the systems. Migration is the most expensive purchase available to a company of this size. It consumes a year of attention and retrains everyone before anything improves. A layer over the systems already running returns in weeks.

Cut into the product. In a service business the relationship is the product. Customers should experience the results of better operations — speed, precision, recall — and never the machinery behind them.

Install a management team. The people running the business generally know things the firm does not. Where a role genuinely needs filling, it gets filled; wholesale replacement is a sign the wrong business was bought.

Run a process on a clock. There is no fund behind OVD Capital and therefore no vintage year and no pressure to exit on a schedule that suits a limited partner rather than the company.

Structure

Own capital, deal by deal.

The principals invest their own money alongside partners they have worked with for twenty years, and they raise co-investment transaction by transaction where a deal warrants it. There is no committed pool awaiting deployment.

The consequence is that the firm says yes rarely and looks closely at what it does say yes to. For a founder weighing offers, it also means the holding period is a commercial judgment rather than a function of when a fund was raised.

This should be understood at the outset rather than discovered later, and it is a fair question to put directly to any of the three.
