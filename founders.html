For founders

A second bite, not an exit.

Most owners approaching this decision want two things that sound contradictory: real money off the table, and a continuing stake in a business they have spent twenty years building. The structure OVD Capital uses is built for exactly that.

Structure

Majority to the firm, a meaningful minority retained.

Typically OVD Capital takes a majority position and the founder retains a meaningful minority. Arrangements have looked like 70/30 or 85/15, though every deal is negotiated on its own facts and neither figure is a template.

The founder takes real liquidity, recognizing what has already been built, and keeps a stake in the value that follows. Where the plan holds, the second bite can exceed the first — which is the entire point of the structure and the reason it is worth being precise about the plan before anyone signs.

What accompanies the capital

  • Funding for modernization, so that the work does not compete with operations for cash.
  • Two operators who have run companies through this exact kind of change, and a third partner with thirty years of the same.
  • A plan that frequently already exists, written with the management team before any transaction was discussed.
Honestly

What OVD Capital is, and is not.

There is no fund behind the firm and no committed pool waiting to be deployed. The principals invest their own capital, deal by deal, alongside partners of twenty years' standing, and raise co-investment where a transaction warrants it.

For most founders this is an advantage rather than a caveat. No ten-year clock. No pressure from limited partners. No forced sale because a vintage is closing and the fund needs to return capital. The holding period is a commercial judgment about the business.

The trade is that the firm moves deliberately and says yes rarely. An owner who needs certainty of close on a compressed timetable is better served by an institutional buyer, and OVD Capital will say so rather than waste the months.

These are the questions worth putting directly to any of the three principals on the first call. None of them will be evaded.

Fit

Where this tends to work.

Profitable, founder-owned, generally $1MM or more in EBITDA. Real customers, proven unit economics, and a brand that means something in its market. Businesses that are sound rather than broken.

The common shape is a company constrained by infrastructure rather than by demand: people spending substantial hours on work that repeats, knowledge residing with a few senior people rather than in any system, and the same information re-entered across three or four applications. Services firms, distribution, light manufacturing, healthcare, financial services and multi-unit operations.

Where that is not the case, the firm will say so on the first call rather than after three months of diligence.

How it usually starts

Frequently with work rather than a term sheet.

Alan Warms and Gary Leff run OVD Advisory, which spends two to three weeks inside a business establishing where AI creates value and what each initiative returns. Many engagements end there, and should.

Where both parties come to agree on what the business could be worth, and both want to pursue it together, the conversation about capital follows. It is a better introduction than a broker's process: each side has seen how the other works before price is discussed.
